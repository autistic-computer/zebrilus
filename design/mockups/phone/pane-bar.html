Pane bar

Inside a pane. The pill gets the machine pill's treatment: every pane of the session is its program's mark, the pane on screen is the capsule with its title over its line, a sideways swipe carries the capsule to the next mark, and a tap on a mark goes to that pane. Panes sit in session order, evenly, with nothing marking where a window ends. When the session has more panes than the row holds it scrolls the same way, cutting the next mark in half under the fade. The hold and its cards go, as the machine pill's hold did: the marks already show every pane and a tap reaches any of them. Three takes on what a mark is. The key row leads with prefix, ctrl and alt, and esc, tab, / and @ fold into one key that opens a menu. Three takes on that key.

Picked: P2 and K1, the key row as sketched

The pill is P2 with the faces at 32, the height of the two lines beside them: 8 in from the capsule's edge, the title 10 after. Off-screen panes keep their 32 face in a 48 tile. The key row is shaped like the bar: a pill holding prefix, ctrl, alt, cmd and the insert key, then the arrow pad and done as discs. The keys share the pill evenly, 4 in from its edge, and a key that is pressed, held, latched or armed shows the capsule's muted wash, 48 tall. ⌘ latches like ctrl and alt and its hold opens Paste, Copy, Find and Select all; the insert key opens K1's chips. Done lowers the keyboard.

P1. Bare marks

As on a machine: each pane is its program's glyph in muted ink, the one on screen in ink. Agents get their own marks (the asterisk, the knot), a terminal running nvim gets nvim's, a plain shell the terminal glyph, a browser the globe. A pane that waits for you has a dot.

P2. Program faces

Each mark is the 24 program face a window card shows, in the program's colors. Reads fastest and matches the machine page's cards, but brings color into the pill.

P3. Tinted marks

P1's bare glyphs in the program's color: the asterisk orange, nvim blue, a shell and a browser in muted ink. Between the two: no tiles, still tells the agents apart at a glance.

The key row

Prefix, ctrl and alt lead, then the insert key, then the arrow pad and hide at the end. Six keys fit any phone, so nothing scrolls and the row loses its fade.

K1. Chips

The insert key (…) opens a stack menu like ctrl's chords: one chip per key, esc nearest the thumb. Tap to open and tap a chip, or press, slide up and let go on a chip in one stroke. The menu closes after each key.

K2. Tray

The insert key opens a tray: one floating row of four 44 keys over it, the key row's own look. Same tap or one-stroke slide as K1, but the keys sit side by side, so the slide is short and sideways.

K3. Last key

K2's tray, but the insert key wears the last key you sent from it, esc at first, with a small dot for the tray behind it. A tap sends that key again, so esc esc is two taps; a hold or a slide up opens the tray.

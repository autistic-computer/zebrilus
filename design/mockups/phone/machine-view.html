Machine view

Today each window is a big tile holding shrunk panes: a chat or a browser shrinks to grey bars, and a terminal to 5pt text. Five takes, each drawn on two days, none with the back button: up is the disc at the bottom left. Today is what the simulator shows on zzios-zebrilus. Busy is a macbook with three sessions: a Claude chat asks to run a command, Codex is working, the tests just passed next to nvim, and a page is open. A and C drop the previews. B and E keep text you can read instead of pictures. D puts what needs you on top.

A. Rows

One row per window under its session's name: the program face with its dot, the title, one line under it. The line is what a preview was for: the ask, the agent's last words, a terminal's last line, the page's host. Tap zooms into the window.

B. Text tiles

Keeps the two-up grid, but each tile shows text at a size you can read instead of a shrunk picture: a terminal's last lines at 12pt, an agent's last message, a page's title and host. A two-pane window spans the row, one column per pane.

C. Small tiles

The Machines tile one level down: 112 tall, two to a row, the window's faces stacked as on a machine tile, its title, and the same one line as A. Waiting tints the tile and puts the word in the corner.

D. Needs you on top

One card above the rows of A for whatever waits on you, with its answer buttons, so you can allow a command without going in. With nothing waiting, the card is the pane you were last on, its last lines readable.

E. Terminal tails

Picked. Only terminals keep a preview, as their last four lines at 13pt across the full width, since a terminal's last lines are what you check from the phone: did it pass, is it done. Chats and pages shrink to one row each.

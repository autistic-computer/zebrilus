A machine

Step 2 of the phone redesign, after Machines: the screen inside a machine. A large title, then a section per session, each a bento grid of window tiles sized by how many panes they hold, as today. The preview is the tile and text is one line: a tile is its status mark and its title over the window's own split, drawn small. Calm windows say nothing more; a window that asks for you or broke adds one line in words, so the ones that want you stand out. Holding a tile, or a session's header, opens a sheet of what you can do to it, with the details the tile leaves out under the sheet's title. There is one way to make something: the pill's menu. The pill names the machine over the session in view, and what its menu makes lands in that session. If the connection drops, the screen zooms back out to the machine's tile, which says Not connected. Every frame is built from the phone components. Next, inside a pane: A pane. Caption text in italics says what the native app does today where it differs.

3. Window tile states

Each tile at its real size. The mark is always there: the warning dot when the window waits for you, the danger dot when it failed or a command exited with an error, the spinner while it works, a dot in the ink when it finished and you have not looked, nothing when idle. Words come only with the warning and danger dots, in their ink (window_summary.rs:464-554), since those are the windows that need you. Working is never green: green only means a machine is connected. Tapping zooms into the pane that needs you, else the active pane; the mini panes are not targets.
